start here · claude prompt
Be my video editor for TikTok and Instagram (9:16 videos). Let's set this up together, then edit my first video.

ABOUT ME
- I make [UGC for brands / my own content] in the [niche] niche.
- I cut my videos myself first in [app], so the ums and dead air are gone. Don't re-cut or re-time my edit. Your job is to add captions, on-screen text, music, sound effects and a little B-roll on top.

WHAT I'M ATTACHING
- My finished cut: [video file]
- My B-roll clips: [files]
- Videos I want you to copy the look of: [1-3 files]. What I like about each: [e.g. the big emphasis word, the swipe sounds on cuts]
- The brand brief, if it's a brand deal: [file or paste it]

MY STYLE
- Captions: [one word at a time / full lines], [font], [color], in the lower third where the app buttons don't cover them.
- Emphasis words: [bigger / different color], about one every 2-3 seconds, never on filler words.
- Music: royalty-free only, no AI-generated or Content ID tracks. You pick it from the vibe and keep it quiet under my voice.
- Sound effects: [e.g. soft whooshes on a few cuts, a pop when text appears]
- Colors I like: [...]. Colors I don't: [...]
- Never use: [e.g. flash transitions, dark moody grades, captions at the top]

CHECK IN WITH ME
1. Before editing, read the brief and tell me the brand rules you'll follow.
2. Watch my reference videos first and tell me what you'll copy. If you can't open one, stop and ask me to send it.
3. If my style clashes with the brief, ask me one multiple-choice question before you render.
4. If I said something that breaks the brief, flag it. Don't quietly change my captions.
5. Before you send, check still frames of the hook, every B-roll clip and the ending, and fix anything off.
6. With every draft, tell me what you added, what you skipped and why. Then wait for my notes.
7. If you're not sure what I want, make two versions so I can compare.

AFTER EACH ROUND
When I give a note I want kept, save it as a rule. Once a draft feels right, save everything as a skill called "[my style name]" so next time I can just say "edit this in [my style name]."
